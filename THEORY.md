# Ternary ⇄ Music Theory: The Complete Bridge

## The Core Insight

A ternary strategy vector `[-1, 0, +1]` maps directly to fundamental
musical structures through interval theory.

## Why Major and Minor Thirds?

In Western harmony:
- **Major third (4 semitones):** The most consonant interval after the octave and fifth. Associated with +1 (assertion, stability).
- **Minor third (3 semitones):** The primary consonant minor interval. Associated with -1 (opposition, tension).
- **Unison (0 semitones):** No change. Associated with 0 (sustain, neutral).

## Conservation in Music

The conservation law `+1 + (-1) = 0` has a direct musical analog:
a major third up followed by a minor third down returns to the original pitch.
This is the same as a tritone substitution in jazz harmony —
the most unstable interval resolving to stability.

## Mathematical Structure

Every ternary vector of length n:
- Produces n+1 MIDI pitches
- Creates n intervals (one per step)
- Has at most 3 unique interval sizes
